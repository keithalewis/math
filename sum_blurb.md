Proving theorems is only table stakes in mathematics.
If a proof is incorrect then it gets tossed in the wastebasket.[^1]
The role of mathematicians is to understand relationships between
mathematical objects. Non-mathematicians seem to be surprised to
learn it is common for mathematicians to invent multiple proofs
of the same theorem. "But it's already been proven! Why waste your time?"

[^1]: "A philosopher's tools are paper and pencil. A mathematician's
tools are paper, pencil, and wastebasket."

It is trivial, and boring, to write a computer program to generate all true
statements satisfied by an axiomatic mathematical system.

> The essence of mathematics is finding proofs of true statements
that __other human beings__ find interesting.

In the mid 1800s George Boole invented an axiomatic system to reduce Aristotelian logic
to mathematical computation, thus laying the groundwork for modern computing.
Starting in the 1930s Alonzo Church invented the $\lambda$-calculus
to rigorously model computation. It took another 40 years for
Haskell Curry and William Howard to show how a program is
a proof and a proof is a program. Now _that_ is interesting!.
